HOW TO DECIDE

Start from the anchors, and from nothing else.

Read the text of every direct anchor and write down, for yourself, what they state in common: a course and perhaps its term, a project, an application to one institution, a matter, an event with a time and a place, one work in several versions, or one purpose the text itself declares. That common statement is the group's basis. If the anchors state nothing in common, or state incompatible things, there is no basis and no group, whatever the engine proposed and whatever the retrieval found: answer "no". If the anchors state too little to tell, answer "insufficient".

Then admit the candidates, one at a time, against that basis and nothing looser. A candidate whose text states the basis, or a value the basis rests on, is included. A candidate whose text contradicts the basis is excluded and named as an outlier, with the kind of contradiction. A candidate that states nothing either way, but is compatible with the basis and reached the dossier through the channel its description names, is uncertain: a person decides, and that is the right outcome for a sparse file. An anchor whose own text contradicts the basis the others state is excluded too.

Then read every flag in "conflicts" against the text, and make sure your outliers account for what the flags point at.

Then, only if the basis holds, name it: a short label a person would recognise for exactly this group, built from what the anchors state, and one category identifier copied from "allowed_vocabulary". The label is a proposal the person confirms or renames.

What a basis is not. A university's name is not a basis: it appears on an essay addressed to the university, a syllabus taught there and a resume that names it as an employer, and those are three relationships, not one. An email domain, a person's name and a shared word are the same kind of thing. A download session is not a basis: files fetched within minutes of each other are worth reading together and prove nothing about each other. A filename with a numbered suffix is not a basis: two files are versions of one work when their text says so. A course code is a basis only within one term: if the anchors state two terms, they are two groups, and you answer "no" rather than merge them. A purpose is a basis only when the text declares it: submission language, a portal, a checklist, a form, an essay that names its target. Content-incoherent files can be purpose-coherent, but only on that kind of evidence.
