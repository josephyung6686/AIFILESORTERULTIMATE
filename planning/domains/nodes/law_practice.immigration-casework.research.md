# Research memo — `law_practice.immigration-casework`

Depth: J-DEPTH
Date: 2026-08-26
Output: `planning/domains/nodes/law_practice.immigration-casework.json`
Roster row: template on the fieldless `law_practice` schema, `parent_id: null`, `launch: placeholder`

## Result

**Accepted, narrowly, after the case against it very nearly carried.** The row survives on one structure the schema's default cannot see (an authorisation-of-representative instrument with a client-consent block, addressed to a government reader) and one privacy rule the default does not contain (the case-type folder level must be seeded ineligible, because it discloses a protected status about a person *without naming them*). It concedes the larger half of its world in two directions and says so on the node.

## The charge — the strongest case that this row should not exist

I state it before the defence, at full strength, because three of its four legs are correct.

**(a) It is a practice area, i.e. a topic label sitting on an unchanged apparatus.** Immigration is how law firms name a department. If this row is admissible, so are family-law casework, employment casework, personal-injury casework and thirty more, each of which files an intake screen, a retainer, correspondence, work product and a closure record on the schema's default signals with different vocabulary in them. That is the 574 failure with a new coat of paint, and the `law_practice` schema itself warns against it: its own text records that the neighbouring landed template "does not recreate practice areas, litigation phases, transaction types, or the dozens of sibling schemas from the overnight catalogue."

**(b) It is a work_type value that already exists.** `law_practice.work_types` contains, verbatim, `"regulatory or registry submission made on a client's behalf"`. An immigration application made on a client's behalf *is* that value. Work types are values on a field, not nodes — the dispatch prompt says so ("Work types are values"), and if the only delta is that the registry happens to be an immigration authority, the delta is a cell in an enum.

**(c) Its evidence is entirely never-alone.** Immigration, visa, asylum, petition, status, permit; USCIS, the Home Office, IRCC, an embassy; a country; a nationality; a case-number-shaped token. `identity.immigration-visa` has already struck every one of these for the applicant side, and its strikes are correct and binding. Strip them and a naive detector has nothing.

**(d) It is `identity.immigration-visa` plus a role flip, and a role name cannot carry a schema.** The applicant's packet is already researched to launch depth. The only claimed difference is that a lawyer holds the bytes — and the schema anchor forecloses that move explicitly: "a practising certificate is a role name and a role name is never-alone."

If I could not answer all four, the honest output was `refuse_node: true` with the coverage routed to `identity.immigration-visa` and Protected Records. Here is why I did not.

**Answer to (a) — and it is the deciding one.** Immigration casework is the single practice area whose file is *not a matter file in the default's sense*. The default's characteristic signals are a litigation-and-transaction apparatus: an intake-and-conflicts screen, a time-and-disbursement export, a limitation-and-listing diary, a disclosure review log, a privilege log, an unexecuted precedent bank, a completion agenda. A clean affirmative immigration case has **no adverse party, no disclosure exercise, no privilege log, no tribunal and often no litigation at all**. What it has instead is a government application workflow: an authority's form set, an evidence index answering a prescribed eligibility list, a fee authorisation, and a sequence of authority-issued notices on the *authority's* clock and under the *authority's* reference. Family-law casework, by contrast, has pleadings, disclosure, hearings and a caption — it *is* the default, and it should be refused if anyone proposes it. This row is not one of thirty interchangeable siblings; it is the one whose apparatus is externally driven. That argument has exactly the shape the schema used one level up against `legal` ("its characteristic files are precisely the ones on which the `legal` schema's instrument-and-proceeding signals DO NOT FIRE"), applied one level down in this row's favour.

**Answer to (b).** A work_type value covers *submitting a form*. It cannot carry a folder-dimension eligibility rule, and it cannot carry a residual split. My two substantive deltas are exactly those two things — the case-type level is seeded ineligible, and a public country-conditions/blank-form library routes to Reading Inbox out of an otherwise wholly protected file. Collapsing this row into an enum cell loses both. The work_type value stays in the schema and is *also* true; it is not a substitute.

**Answer to (c).** Conceded in full, and encoded rather than argued around. The node's `never_alone` list strikes the vocabulary, the authority names, the countries, the reference-shaped tokens, the practitioner role names, the folder names, the download session, and — a strike this row adds — cross-applicant semantic similarity, which is guaranteed to fire falsely here because the authority prescribes the same evidence list to every applicant. It also strikes the blank public form, where *emptiness of the applicant slots is disqualifying evidence, not weak evidence*.

**Answer to (d).** The discriminator is never "a lawyer holds it." It is two observable structures the applicant's own copy cannot have: (i) a form whose printed parts split a representative block from a represented-person block and add a **third, separately signed consent block** — the United States Form G-28 *Notice of Entry of Appearance as Attorney or Accredited Representative*, the Canadian IMM 5476 *Use of a Representative*, a United Kingdom application's immigration-adviser block; and (ii) an authority notice carrying a **separate representative delivery block** and, characteristically, **two references in two labelled slots** — the authority's and the representative's own file reference. Read through 00's direct-fact path, "a labeled form field". These are structures, not role names, and they survive the schema's deletion test.

## The node test, all three legs, against the schema's DEFAULT TEMPLATE

The `law_practice` default I am measured against, read from `law_practice.json`: activation requires *both* an exact matter/file/engagement reference repeated across two or more artefacts *and* an artefact whose labelled slots separate a practitioner-or-firm role from a client role; the recommended order is client (ineligible unless multi-client and explicitly approved) → matter → document function → period, not time-first; the privacy default is `potentially_sensitive` because a third party who cannot consent is exposed; the residual profile is Protected Records first, then Review Later, then Unsupported or Encrypted, with the depersonalised precedent bank as the single Reading Inbox exception.

**Leg 1 — detection signals differ.** The default's anchor is a **firm-allocated** matter reference. This row's anchor is an **authority-issued** case or receipt reference, legible simultaneously to the authority, the applicant and the representative, and it is paired with the authorisation instrument described above. Three of the default's signals cannot fire on a clean file here at all (disclosure review, privilege log, completion agenda), and this row replaces the bundle-index signal with a structurally different one: an **evidence index keyed to a numbered requirement or tab**, with no responsive/not-responsive coding column, no privilege column and no control-number range. The multi-applicant tracker is a portfolio table over **people**, where the default's diary is a portfolio table over **matters**. Different signals, on different files. Passes.

**Leg 2 — recommended dimensions differ.** Both orders serialise as `[]` under PR-6, so the difference must be argued in the prose, and it is real. The default holds the *client* level ineligible because a visible client name discloses that a representation exists. This row inherits that and adds a **second ineligible level the default does not have**: the case type. A folder named asylum, removal defence, trafficking, or domestic-violence self-petition states that the human being underneath it is a claimed refugee, is facing removal, or is a victim of trafficking or domestic violence — *and it does so without naming them, so it survives redaction of the name*. That is a different disclosure mechanism from the default's (status, not relationship) and it attaches to the folder level rather than the file. The rest of the order is inherited unchanged, including 00's parent-must-make-the-child-intelligible rule — "A work type such as Homework 3 is meaningful only after the course is known" — which holds here because a response index is meaningless without the request it answers. Not time-first: "For document and record domains, project, function, or subject usually comes before time because putting year first scatters related work across calendar folders," and filing date, priority date, receipt date, appointment date and decision date all differ within one case. Passes.

**Leg 3 — privacy rules differ.** The default protects a third party who cannot consent. This row adds three things: the third party is by definition a non-citizen whose right to remain is the file's subject; in protection work the file *states the ground of the claim in labelled slots and numbered paragraphs* and names family members who may still be in the country of origin, so exposure can endanger people who are not in this filesystem; and the sensitivity survives name redaction, which is what forces the dimension rule in Leg 2. Two operational consequences follow that the default does not carry: recognition here reads document **grammar** and refuses to read narrative content, and cross-applicant similarity is suppressed as a grouping signal. Passes, and most clearly.

Three legs, three independent differences. Accept.

## Files considered and rejected

Naming what is *not* this row's evidence was the larger half of the work.

- **`Blank I-130 form and instructions - downloaded.pdf`.** The most tempting false positive in the row: authority letterhead, form identifier, edition date, complete labelled-slot layout, the entire vocabulary. It has no person in it. Kept in the JSON as a fixture precisely so the disqualifying rule is written down — empty applicant slots are *disqualifying*, not weak. Reading Inbox.
- **`Immigration Law Practice Manual - AILA - 2026 edition.pdf`.** Professional-body name, practitioner audience, legal-services context, whole subject vocabulary. Nothing structural survives the deletion test. Reading Inbox. This is the fixture the "organisation name is never-alone" strike exists for.
- **The client's own passport scan.** Routine in this corpus because identity verification is a mandatory part of matter opening, but it is `identity`'s on `identity`'s own evidence and stays protected there. Membership in a case never converts an identity document into a case fact, and never licenses reading it into a prompt.
- **An applicant's own copy of a decision letter or permit.** `identity.immigration-visa`'s. Rejected as this row's evidence unless a representative structure is present on the page or an accepted authorisation instrument is co-present.
- **A university international office's enrolment certificate issued for a visa application.** `academic.study-abroad` / `identity.immigration-visa` have already argued this reciprocally and correctly; this row adds nothing and takes nothing.
- **A travel booking, itinerary or boarding pass in the same folder as a visa.** `travel.bookings-confirmations`. Date and destination adjacency is not evidence.
- **A practice-management or document-management system's live database or mail account.** A source system, not a file node. Only a bounded export with a readable manifest is represented.
- **A contacts export containing applicants, sponsors, interpreters and authority staff.** Names are never-alone; a relationship role needs evidence inside a workflow artefact.
- **An accepted-group folder named "Immigration".** A user folder name is a purpose *clue*. 00 permits it as weak support for purpose, not as an anchor, and here it is worse than usual because the word saturates the corpus.

## The collision fixtures

Three, each with the same bytes on both sides.

1. **`Biometrics appointment notice - ASC Newark - 2026-09-14.pdf`** — the primary one. Authority header, appointment slots, applicant slot, what-to-bring list. With no representative delivery block it is **`identity.immigration-visa`'s**, full stop; the authority sends the applicant their own copy and that copy is the neighbour's normal case. The same notice carrying an attorney-of-record copy line, or sitting beside an accepted authorisation instrument for the named person, is this row's. What discriminates: the delivery block and the two-reference header — nothing else on the page differs.
2. **`Country Policy and Information Note - Nigeria - sexual orientation and gender identity.pdf`** — byte-identical to a public download, no person named, no reference slot, no addressee. It is Reading Inbox material. The dangerous inference is the *reverse* one, and the node records it: never conclude a client's ground of claim from what background material sits next to their file. Topical proximity is not membership; only an exact case reference in an accompanying index is.
3. **`H-1B petition copy and LCA - Ramirez - HR file.pdf`** — the employer-side fixture. Same document class, different holder: the two-role split is employer-and-employee, the attesting party on the wage attestation is the employer itself, there is no authorisation instrument, and the file sits in a personnel set. `hr.onboarding-offboarding`'s.

A fourth is worth naming even though it is not in the fixture list as a competitor: **`Retainer - immigration services - flat fee - executed.pdf`** is not distinctive to this row at all. It is the *schema default's* engagement-terms artefact, and its bound party pair plus execution block also fires `legal`. Including it honestly is part of showing where this row stops.

## Reciprocal boundaries

Stated in both directions, same fixture named on both sides. Five collisions authored: `identity.immigration-visa`, `legal`, `legal.practice-matter-file`, `hr.onboarding-offboarding`, `government.constituent-casework`. Every id verified present in `planning/domains/roster.json`.

The one I want to flag for R1c is **`government.constituent-casework`**, because it is the most deceptive and I nearly missed it. A legislator's or ombudsman's office holds a *signed authorisation naming their office to act for a named individual with an immigration authority*, plus the authority's reply correspondence — the same tri-partite structure this row calls decisive. The discriminator is what the authorisation authorises: a constituent privacy release authorises an **inquiry**, this row's instrument enters a representative on the record for a **filing**. Corroborating, in the corpus rather than on the page: a filing corpus contains a prepared form set, an evidence index and a fee authorisation; an inquiry corpus contains none of them. Where the surrounding corpus is absent, abstain to Review Later rather than choose.

Co-activations (`also_holds_with`): `identity`, `legal`, `finance` — all one-way, all owing R1c a reciprocal, all on disjoint evidence, and in each case the safety domain's protection runs first per "Finance, identity, medical, and legal material should be implemented first as safety domains, meaning the system detects and protects them before any cloud or automated placement decision is allowed."

**Neighbours considered that did not get an edge.** `identity.core-documents` — the passport-versus-permit seam is already drawn by `identity.immigration-visa` and duplicating it here would add nothing. `applications.purpose-packet` — the admissions-versus-immigration ambiguity is likewise already argued on the neighbour, and this row's discriminator (the authorisation instrument) is orthogonal to it. `academic.study-abroad` — same reason. `travel.*` — a trip group never supplies immigration facts and the seam is the neighbour's, already stated. `medical` — a required medical or vaccination certificate is `medical`'s on its own evidence and neither schema erases the other, but the confusion is not same-evidence, so it is a coactivation case recorded in a fixture rather than a mutex.

## Fields and dimensions

`fields: []`, `proposed_fields: []`, `template.dimension_order: []`, `time_first: false`. All intentional: `law_practice` declares no field rows under PR-6, a template may only reuse its schema's fields, and a dimension may only branch on a declared field.

One candidate was genuinely tempting and is deliberately **not** minted: an **authority-issued case reference**, distinct from a firm-allocated matter reference. It is the strongest grouping anchor in this world and no canonical key covers it (`institution` and `record_type` are Finance-scoped; `project` and `artifact_type` are Research and Code; `client` and `our_firm` are engagement roles, not references). I record it here as a deferred candidate for R1c rather than proposing it, on two grounds: the schema declares no fields at all, so a template minting one would invert the contract; and a serialised authority reference is itself a disclosure token, so whether it may exist as a *field* is entangled with whether it may appear in a *label*, which is NJ-4 territory. `case_type` is deliberately not proposed at all — the whole privacy argument of this row is that it must never become a level.

## Open questions — NEEDS-JOSEPH

**NJ-1 — Detained and removal work.** Removal, appeal and judicial-review documents carry a tribunal caption, so `legal` fires and its safety protection runs first. Alternatives: (i) this row holds only affirmative administrative applications and hands captioned proceedings wholly to `legal`; (ii) this row stays active across the non-captioned apparatus of a captioned case (the authorisation form, the evidence index, the tracker) with `legal` co-active on the captioned members; (iii) detained work becomes its own row. I have written (ii) into the node because it matches the schema's own concession idiom, but it is not settled by any design doc and it decides whether the most sensitive sub-world in this domain has a home.

**NJ-2 — A landed neighbour's edge now points at the wrong sibling.** `identity.immigration-visa`'s `collides_with` entry for the practice side names `legal.practice-matter-file`, and its stated practice-side discriminator is "a client-matter or engagement reference, practitioner letterhead and fee-earner role, filing cover sheet, advice memorandum, or multi-client case-management export." That row predates this one. Alternatives for R1c: re-point the entry here; duplicate it so both practice-side rows are named; or leave it and accept that the immigration-specific discriminator (the authorisation instrument) lives only on this side. **Recommendation to R1c, not an edit** — I did not touch that file.

**NJ-3 — Non-lawyer representatives.** The same authorisation instrument is signed by accredited non-lawyer representatives, regulated consultants and nonprofit caseworkers, who produce a byte-identical corpus while not practising law. Alternatives: keep them on `law_practice` (the structure matches, the profession does not); route them to a nonprofit or `business_operations` schema (the profession matches, the structure does not); or leave them uncovered. The third is a real loss — this is a large filing world — and the first is what the node currently implies by describing the instrument generically ("attorney, accredited representative, adviser or agent"). Joseph should decide whether `law_practice` is defined by the apparatus or by the profession, because this row cannot be both.

**NJ-4 — Ineligibility has no serialised home.** This row's substantive contribution to the template layer is a *negative* rule: the case-type dimension must never be created. Under PR-6 there is no field to mark ineligible, so the rule survives only as prose in `template.why`, where nothing enforces it and no later phase can read it. Alternatives: accept prose-only until fields land; give the contract a serialised ineligible-dimension list independent of fields; or push the rule into P7 as a handling policy. This is not specific to immigration — the `law_practice` default has the same problem with the client level — but it bites hardest here, because the disclosure survives name redaction.

## Final recommendation

Keep `law_practice.immigration-casework` as a placeholder template with no fields, no serialised dimensions and no time-first hierarchy. Activate only on the authorisation-of-representative structure or an authority notice carrying a representative delivery block; concede the applicant's own copy to `identity.immigration-visa` and every captioned document to `legal`; group only on an exact authority reference plus an accepted authorisation instrument, never on dossier composition or similarity; read grammar and never narrative; and treat the case-type folder level as permanently ineligible.
