# Research memo — `government.transport-authority`

Date: 2026-08-26
Depth: J-DEPTH
Output: `planning/domains/nodes/government.transport-authority.json`
Roster row: template on the fieldless `government` schema, `parent_id: null`, placeholder launch

## Result

**Accept**, narrowly, and not for the reason the roster hint gives. The hint describes a transport
authority's whole in-tray — "service specifications and contracts, timetables and fares, asset and
highway records, works notices and passenger casework" — and four of those five clauses are other
people's rows. The row survives on a much smaller base than the hint claims: the **network object
itself**, in its authority-side editorial form, plus the movement data generated by operating it.
Everything else in the hint is routed to a neighbour below, reciprocally.

## The charge, argued first

The strongest case against this row is that it is an **organisation name** — never-alone evidence
by the government schema's own rule, which excludes "a government department, regulator,
municipality, legislature, court, public school, archive, museum, or official-looking seal alone".
"Transport authority" is precisely such a name. Stated at full strength, the case against is:

1. **It is an industry, not a filing world.** The government schema already refuses activation on
   "a public-sector employer name". A transport authority is a public body that happens to be about
   buses. If "transport" is doing the work, the row is a topic word.
2. **It is a union of neighbours.** Walk the hint: *service specifications and contracts* →
   `government.public-procurement`; *works notices* → `government.permit-licensing`; *passenger
   casework* → `government.constituent-casework`; *asset and highway records* →
   `construction_property.construction-project` and `engineering.civil-structural`; the committee
   packs that carry all of it → `government.municipal-administration`. A row assembled entirely
   from other rows' fixtures is a browse label.
3. **Its dimensions are identical to its schema default.** PR-6 leaves `government` fieldless.
   `dimension_order` is `[]` here and `[]` in `government.json`. The second leg of the node test
   returns *no difference*, flatly.
4. **Much of its evidence is deliberately public**, which is the government schema's own strongest
   negative: "a law, regulation, judgment, government report, official statistics release, meeting
   minutes, consultation paper, or tender notice downloaded for reference; publication by
   government is not authority-side custody". Timetables, maps and open feeds are the most-published
   artefacts any authority produces. If the row's headline evidence is published, it is a reading
   pile.

That is a serious case. Points 2 and 3 are conceded in full below. The row is accepted only because
points 1 and 4 fail against two specific, nameable things.

### What defeats it

**(a) A file family whose *structure* is the evidence, not the organisation's name.** A public
transport interchange feed is not a document about transport; it is a fixed set of named member
tables — `agency.txt`, `stops.txt`, `routes.txt`, `trips.txt`, `stop_times.txt`, `calendar.txt`,
`fare_attributes.txt`, `shapes.txt` — that only exists because a network exists. The XML dialects
used in Europe (a scheduled-service exchange schema declared at the root element; the UK bus
registration dialect; the realtime sibling served as protocol-buffer payloads) are the same fact in
another syntax. Nothing else on the roster produces that shape. This is a deterministic signal in
the strict sense the government default asks for — repeated structured slots, not vocabulary — and
it is **absent from the government schema's deterministic list**, which enumerates bill packets,
rulemaking packets, decision records, procurement records, governance cycles, statistics packets,
election packets, case-management exports and office mail. A feed is none of those nine.

The same holds for three further authority-side shapes the default does not enumerate: a **traffic
regulation order** in draft — schedule of restrictions, statement of reasons, notice of proposal,
objection register, sealing block — which is not a decision *about an applicant* and therefore not
the default's "decision record"; a **standing asset register with an inspection cycle**, whose
discriminating slot is `next due` against a prior inspection of the same structure identifier; and
a **noticing register spanning many promoters**, whose discriminating property is that no single
applicant owns it.

**(b) A privacy rule that is different in kind, not merely in degree.** Every sensitive holding the
government schema anticipates belongs to someone who *interacted with the authority*: casework
subjects, requesters, bidders, respondents, evaluators, voters. A transport authority's most
re-identifying holdings belong to people who did nothing but travel — smartcard and account-based
ticketing extracts that reconstruct a person's week, vehicle-location and passenger-counting
traces, enforcement camera packages with readable plates, casualty records with age and injury
attributes. Those people never filed anything. That is a new class of subject for this schema.

Point 4 inverts into the second half of the same rule. Because the published feed and the ticketing
extract are produced by the *same system* and land in the *same directory*, this row needs a
never-alone the default does not have: **the presence of openly published network data must never
lower the posture of the movement data beside it.** The government default has a uniform
`potentially_sensitive` posture; this row has a split one that must be defended file-by-file.

### Node test, leg by leg

- **Detection signals — DIFFER.** Ten deterministic signals, of which the feed-structure, service
  specification, fare instrument, network order, multi-promoter noticing register, cyclical asset
  register, count/collision dataset and telemetry signals have no counterpart in
  `government.json`'s list. Twelve never-alone entries, of which four are specific to this world
  (downloaded published feed; traveller's ticket or journey history; recipient's vehicle or licence
  document; operator's roster quoting the specification).
- **Dimension order — DOES NOT DIFFER.** Both are `[]`. PR-6 makes this leg mute for every
  government child, and I decline to manufacture a difference here. The prose shape is recorded in
  `template.why` for later adjudication and nothing is serialized.
- **Privacy rules — DIFFER.** New subject class (the untransacting traveller) and a split
  directory-level posture, as argued above.

Two of three legs differ, one is structurally unavailable. That is the honest result and the row is
accepted on it.

## The collision fixture

`google_transit.zip` is in the JSON **twice, deliberately**, with identical member structure and
opposite verdicts. It is the sharpest collision on the row because the bytes can be literally
identical: the same agency, the same stops, the same trips.

- **Mine:** `google_transit_2026-09-01_draft.zip` — an *unpublished* revision, sitting in a dated
  release series beside a scheduling-system export and a validator error report naming the holder's
  own release. The editorial apparatus is the custody evidence.
- **Not mine:** `google_transit.zip` — a portal download beside routing experiments and map tiles.
  No draft, no series, no validator run, no custodian metadata. → Reading Inbox, and `also_schema:
  code` when it is being consumed by software.

**What discriminates:** never the feed's contents, never the agency name inside `agency.txt` —
always the presence or absence of the *production* side. A consumer holds one snapshot; a producer
holds drafts, a series, and its own errors. This is the concrete form of the government schema's
rule that publication by government is not authority-side custody.

A second, quieter collision: `Smartcard transaction extract - concession scheme - 2026-07.csv`
(mine, Protected) versus `My travel account - journey history July 2026.pdf` (not mine). Both are
tap sequences. The discriminator is ticketing-**system** provenance and many card tokens versus
account-holder branding and one named person at a home address.

## Fields and dimensions

`fields: []`, `proposed_fields: []`, `dimension_order: []`, `time_first: false`. PR-6 leaves
`government` fieldless and a template may not mint what its schema does not declare. Candidates
considered and **not** proposed, because minting them here would pre-empt central adjudication:
an instrument/contract-package reference, a standing-asset identifier, a feed-release identifier.
Existing canonical keys were checked and rejected — `record_type` and `institution` are scoped to
Finance, `project` to Research and Code, `artifact_type` to Code. Nothing canonical fits a network
anchor, and I decline to stretch one. Carried as NJ-TRANS-1.

`time_first` is false and the reason is concrete for this row: a timetable world tempts a year-first
tree, and a year-first tree would split one route's specification, its orders, its inspections and
its counts across a decade of calendar folders — exactly what the design warns of when it says
"putting year first scatters related work across calendar folders".

## NEEDS-JOSEPH

- **NJ-TRANS-1** — PR-6 keeps `government` fieldless, so this row's organizing anchors exist only as
  prose. Decide centrally whether an authority-side bounded-instrument reference, a standing-asset
  identifier, and a feed-release identifier may exist as fields, and whether any is
  destination-eligible. Alternatives: (a) keep fieldless indefinitely and accept that every
  government child recommends nothing; (b) mint one generic bounded-reference key shared across all
  government children; (c) mint per-child keys, which reopens the 574's expansion.
- **NJ-TRANS-2** — Decide whether a deliberately published feed, map, or made order inside an
  authority corpus may be marked non-sensitive independently of its neighbours. Alternatives:
  (a) per-file posture, which risks a summary of the open feed leaking the directory's existence to
  a remote model alongside the ticketing extract; (b) directory-level posture dominates, which is
  what this row currently assumes and which over-protects genuinely public bytes.
- **NJ-TRANS-3** — Reciprocity check owed to `government.public-consultation` and
  `government.public-procurement`: both are landed-or-pending siblings I have claimed a fixture
  against without reading their rows. R1c should confirm the boundary is symmetric, and in
  particular that public-procurement agrees the surviving specification is mine.
- **NJ-TRANS-4** — Confirm the deliberate non-edge to `engineering.automotive-program` is symmetric
  and not an omission; its memo asserts "no shared discriminating evidence from this side" and I
  agree from mine.
- **NJ-TRANS-5** — The holder-role seam this row depends on (authority specifies, operator runs)
  does not exist where a state-owned or arm's-length company does both. Decide whether such a body
  activates this row, `business_operations`, or neither, and whether it may be user-confirmed.

## Final recommendation

Keep `government.transport-authority` as a placeholder template with no fields, no dimensions, no
schema coactivation edge, and no time-first hierarchy. Its licence to exist is narrow and should
stay narrow: the **authority-side editorial form of the network** — draft feeds and their release
apparatus, service specifications that outlive their competitions, network orders, multi-promoter
coordination registers, cyclical asset inspection, and the measurement and movement data the
network generates. Everything the roster hint listed beyond that has been handed to a named
neighbour with the fixture named on both sides. If R1c finds that the feed-structure signal and the
untransacting-traveller privacy rule do not survive review, this row should be re-refused and its
coverage routed to `government.municipal-administration`, `government.public-procurement`,
`government.permit-licensing` and Independent Records — the case for that outcome is written above
in full and was not smoothed away.
