# Research memo — `hr.dei-program`

Date: 2026-08-26
Depth: J-DEPTH
Output: `planning/domains/nodes/hr.dei-program.json`
Roster row: template on the fieldless `hr` schema, `parent_id: null`, `launch: placeholder`, `provenance: proposal`

## Result

**Accept the node**, narrowed. It survives the charge on two of the three node-test legs — detection signals and privacy rules — and survives the third only as prose. It is narrowed in two ways the roster hint did not state: it excludes DEI-*topic* artefacts (training courses, external publications, vendor material) and it excludes supply-base diversity, keeping only evidence about the organisation's **own workforce composition** joined to a monitoring, declaration, or reporting structure.

The row's real content is the protected-characteristic instrument and its downstream reporting family. That family has slots no other `hr` sibling names, and it carries a disclosure risk the `hr` default explicitly gets backwards. Both are argued below.

## The charge — the strongest case that this row should not exist

I ran the charge before writing anything, and four of the five attacks land hard.

**Attack 1 — it is a work_type value of its own schema.** This is the strongest and it is documented in my own anchor. `planning/domains/nodes/hr.json` already lists, verbatim in its `work_types` array, `"DEI strategy, monitoring, programme, or reporting record"`. The `hr` schema therefore already carries this coverage as an *enum value*. The stamped prompt says plainly: "`work_types[]` is an enum of values for a `work_type` (or equivalent) field. Do not ask R1a for a child node per work type." On its face this row is precisely the mistake the prompt forbids.

**Attack 2 — it is a topic, and topic is not purpose.** "Diversity" is a subject word. `00` uses the word exactly once, and not in this sense: purpose detection asks "whether the group is purpose-coherent despite topic diversity". That is the only occurrence of any of diversity / equity / inclusion / DEI / protected characteristic in `00` (grep-verified across the whole file; the single hit is the purpose-detection paragraph). A row whose name is a topic label, with no support in the authority document, is exactly a label pretending to be a filing world.

**Attack 3 — duplicate of `hr.workforce-analytics`.** A representation table cut by unit and grade is a workforce analysis with one extra column. If the only difference between two rows is *which column* the spreadsheet carries, that is a value, not a node.

**Attack 4 — duplicate of `business_operations.policy-handbook` and of `hr.engagement-survey`.** The equal-opportunities policy is a policy; the inclusion index is a cut of an engagement wave. Strip those two away and the row may be hollow.

**Attack 5 — defined by never-alone evidence.** DEI-worded files are overwhelmingly marketing, ESG, syllabi, vendor decks, and job adverts. If the word is the evidence, the row can never activate.

### Why the charge is defeated (and where it partly succeeds)

Attacks 2 and 5 are conceded as *constraints*, not as refutations: they are why the JSON's `never_alone` list leads with the DEI vocabulary itself and with the DEI-titled training certificate, and why the topic-only fixtures (`2026 Sustainability Report - Our People section.pdf`, `Unconscious bias training - completion roster - Q2 2026.csv`) are written into the file set as things this row must *refuse*. A row that refuses its own name-words is not a label row.

Attack 1 fails on a distinction the prompt itself draws. `work_type` is a value; a *template* is a recommended dimension order plus detection signals plus privacy rules for one organisational situation. "DEI reporting record" being a legal value of `work_type` does not settle whether the situation that produces those records has its own signals and privacy posture — and it does. The decisive artefact is not a report at all: it is the **voluntary self-identification instrument**, a form whose defining slot is an explicit non-response option (*I do not wish to answer*, *prefer not to say*, *decline to state*) beside a voluntariness or lawful-basis paragraph. Real named instances: the US **Form CC-305, Voluntary Self-Identification of Disability**; the race/ethnicity and veteran-status self-ID pages in the same family; UK equality-monitoring forms held separately from the personnel record. That slot appears essentially nowhere else in an HR corpus — an ordinary personnel form asks for a date of birth and a department, and does not offer a refusal option with an explanation of why refusing is safe. It is a detection signal that is *true of my file list* and false of every other `hr` sibling's.

Attacks 3 and 4 fail on scope in both directions. This row is **wider** than analytics (it holds a declaration instrument, an equality plan with availability and utilisation analysis, and a network charter — none of which are measurement) and **narrower** (it does not hold headcount, attrition, absence, cost, or span-of-control analysis with no characteristic dimension). Against `policy-handbook` the seam is one the anchor already draws for the whole schema: `hr.json`'s `needs_llm` says the governing policy "belongs to business_operations" while the employee-specific application may activate HR. This row inherits that seam and applies it to equality policy specifically. Against `engagement-survey` the seam is the instrument: the wave, its questionnaire and its raw response file belong to the sibling; a demographic cut computed *for monitoring* is this row's, and the fixture is marked `group_without_copying_facts: true` so the cut can join a monitoring group without importing the wave's facts.

Attack 3 is nonetheless the one I cannot fully close, and it is recorded as **NJ-DEI-2** rather than smoothed.

## Node test, argued in full

The `hr` schema's **default template**, quoted from the anchor's `template.why`, is: *work type or named people programme first; then workforce unit or cohort when the corpus genuinely spans several; then people cycle; and only under explicit user policy a pseudonymous personnel case.* `time_first` is false. Its default privacy posture is `potentially_sensitive` on employee-identifying content, and its default signal shape is "a workforce population, personnel process, or employee case".

**Leg 1 — detection signals: DIFFER.** Four signals in the JSON have no counterpart in the anchor's list. (i) The self-declaration instrument with a non-response option and a voluntariness paragraph. (ii) The fixed-shape statutory return — job-category × race/ethnicity × sex counts with an establishment identifier (EEO-1 Component 1), or mean/median hourly and bonus gap with a four-quartile distribution and a signed accuracy statement (a UK-style gender pay gap report). (iii) The availability / incumbency-versus-availability / placement-goal section structure of an affirmative-action plan. (iv) The paired outcome-and-group-flag analysis producing a per-group ratio or regression residual (adverse-impact and pay-equity analyses). The anchor's nearest signal is a single clause about "an engagement, DEI, or workforce-analytics export whose labelled population cuts and measures explicitly describe the organisation's own workforce" — one clause covering three siblings, which is a gist, not these four structures. Leg 1 passes.

**Leg 2 — privacy rules: DIFFER, and materially.** The anchor's clause reads "Aggregation may reduce disclosure, but does not by itself prove the HR reading." That is a statement about *activation*. For this row the aggregation problem is a **disclosure** problem and it runs the opposite way: in a diversity cross-tab, the smaller the cell the more disclosive it is, and suppressed cells are routinely reconstructable from row and column margins. A rule that treats "no names present" as licence to summarise remotely is unsafe here and safe for most of `hr`. Add the separate-holding expectation attached to voluntary declarations — the instrument itself tells the employee it is held apart from the personnel file — and this row must forbid a join that the rest of `hr` would treat as ordinary enrichment. Leg 2 passes, and it is the strongest leg.

**Leg 3 — recommended dimensions: DIFFER in intent, but cannot be serialized.** Under PR-6 and D1 the `hr` schema declares no field rows, so `dimension_order` is `[]` and no proposed key may become a folder level. The intended difference is recorded in `template.why`: lead with the reporting or programme instance rather than work type, and treat `workforce_unit` as **not** visible by default — the inverse of the schema default, because a folder tree branching on business unit or site publishes small-population composition at exactly the granularity that re-identifies people. An employee-name level is forbidden outright, since the branch label would disclose a declared characteristic. Leg 3 is honestly a prose difference only; it does not carry the verdict, and legs 1 and 2 do not need it.

Verdict: `refuse_node: false`. Two independent legs differ; a third differs in intent. This is not a second copy of the `hr` default template.

## The collision fixture

**`2026 Sustainability Report - Our People section.pdf`.** It looks exactly like this row's evidence: DEI vocabulary throughout, representation percentages in pull-quotes, an assurance statement, a reporting period. It is not this row's evidence.

Three things discriminate it: it contains **no workforce rows** (narrative and photography, not a population table); it contains **no declaration instrument** and no fixed statutory return structure; and its **audience is external** — an assurance statement and a framework index are addressed to investors, not to a people function. A monitoring export always carries the population; an external publication carries only the conclusion. It routes to Reading Inbox when no accepted programme is active, and it may legitimately co-activate `business_operations` as a published organisational record — which is why it is marked `also_schema: business_operations` rather than simply discarded.

## Fields, proposed fields, and dimensions

`fields: []`, `proposed_fields: []`, `dimension_order: []`, `time_first: false`. All intentional.

The row proposes **no** keys, and reuses the anchor's pending proposals rather than minting variants: a snapshot or reporting instance is `people_cycle` ("named or bounded HR process instance"); a population cut is `workforce_unit`; an individual subject is `workforce_member`. The DEI work types are values of `work_type`, which is the honest concession to Attack 1.

One candidate was considered and **deliberately rejected**: `protected_characteristic`. It is rejected on principle, not on availability. A field whose *values are themselves special-category data* must never become a stored fact, a search facet, or a folder label; minting it would create the exact artefact — a browsable characteristic dimension — that this row's privacy analysis exists to prevent. The distinction this row needs is "a characteristic column is present", which is a recognition signal, not a fact about the file's subject.

## Residual routing

**Protected Records** is the principal fallback, on `00`'s own terms — it "may represent sensitive isolated material … it should normally remain local-only and must not cause filenames or content to be exposed in model prompts" — and takes declaration instruments, monitoring exports, pay-equity and adverse-impact analyses, and equality plans when no accepted group is active. **Independent Records** takes a published pay-gap report or a network charter: `00`, "standalone certificates, notices, confirmations, forms, and PDFs that have a durable purpose but no broader group." **Review Later** takes DEI-shaped artefacts whose population is unresolved (the benchmark-versus-own-workforce case); **Reading Inbox** takes external publications, benchmarks and vendor material; **Unsupported or Encrypted** takes the password-protected archive without cracking or unpacking it; **Temporary Screenshots** takes a positively-evidenced dashboard capture when OCR establishes no group. A download session that brings several DEI-worded files together proves nothing: `00` — "A tight download session alone is never sufficient: it is a retrieval clue that may bring the files together, but not proof of their shared purpose."

## NEEDS-JOSEPH

**NJ-DEI-1 — Where do statutory equality returns live?** Alternatives: (a) here, grouped with the extract and analysis that produced them, protected by default; (b) `business_operations.corporate-regulatory-filings`, grouped with the organisation's other submissions; (c) both, via a coactivation the placeholder cannot author. The stakes are real: (b) risks a compulsory-filing artefact inheriting a low-sensitivity posture, when the return is built from characteristic data. This row currently assumes (a) with `also_schema: business_operations` on the fixture. Reciprocal note owed to `business_operations.corporate-regulatory-filings`.

**NJ-DEI-2 — Does this row survive as distinct from `hr.workforce-analytics`?** This is the surviving half of Attack 3. Alternatives: (a) keep both, with the seam at "is a protected-characteristic dimension present"; (b) merge into `workforce-analytics` and carry the DEI privacy rules there as a conditional posture; (c) keep both but move the declaration instrument and statutory return to this row and *all* measurement to analytics, leaving this row programme-and-instrument only. Option (c) is the cleanest and I recommend R1c consider it. I did not take it unilaterally because it would require an edit to a neighbour's row.

**NJ-DEI-3 — Suppression policy.** Should the product refuse to summarise, index, or preview a cell below a base size, and if so, who owns that rule — P7 handling, this catalogue, or user policy? No threshold is stated here by design. The related question is whether margin-based reconstruction of suppressed cells must be actively prevented or merely not performed.

**NJ-DEI-4 — Separate-holding.** Voluntary declarations are collected on an explicit promise that they are held apart from the personnel record. Should the product enforce a non-join barrier between a declaration corpus and the payroll/personnel corpus, and can P9 represent a *prohibited* edge rather than only permitted ones?

**NJ-DEI-5 — Jurisdictional shape.** The fixtures deliberately mix US-shaped (EEO-1, CC-305, affirmative-action plan) and UK-shaped (pay-gap snapshot with quartiles) instruments to show the structural family, not to assert either regime. If R4 ever gazetteers reporting instruments, decide whether that is a catalogue this row consumes or an invented list this row must refuse. No instrument list is invented here.
