# creative.licensing-rights — J-DEPTH R1b research memo

**J-DEPTH:** FULL R1b DEPTH (ratified 2026-08-24), not gist.
**Verdict:** REFUSE this creative template and reroute by record function.
**JSON-first:** `creative.licensing-rights.json` was authored before this memo.
**Launch:** placeholder; `fields: []`; no canonical or proposed field rows.
**Core boundary:** executed grants/releases → `legal`; royalty/payment records → `finance`; creative assets stay `creative`.
**Node-test result:** creative work is subject matter, and licensor/licensee are party roles; neither creates a creative-specific template.
**Safety:** potentially sensitive; releases, signatures, commercial terms and royalty income require protection before model use or placement.

## Sources and method

The authority stack used was `planning/00-database-agent-product-design.md`, `planning/01-product-design-structured.md`, `planning/prompts/ALIGNMENT.md`, `planning/domains/_CONTRACT.md`, `planning/domains/CONNECTION.md`, `planning/domains/CONNECTION-EXAMPLES.md`, `planning/domains/canonical_fields.json`, `planning/domains/roster.json`, `planning/domains/ROSTER.md`, `planning/overnight/council/DECISION-BRIEF.md`, and `src/evidence_shape/vocabulary.py`. The stamped assignment was generated with `python3 planning/domains/dispatch/make_prompt.py creative.licensing-rights`. Comparison rows were the landed `creative` schema, `creative.client-engagement`, `legal`, `legal.practice-matter-file`, `finance`, `identity`, and `photos` nodes. `creative.deliverable-handoff` was checked as a roster neighbour; where it was unavailable as a landed node, only its roster-defined organizational situation was used and no claims were attributed to a missing file.

The governing design makes a file a record with many facts, not one permanent category. It separates observations from facts, schema activation from grouping, and schemas from organizational templates. It also requires small schemas and permits refusal where a proposed template merely repeats another schema or rests on work types, extensions, names or other never-alone values. The creative schema is a placeholder and declares no fields. This row therefore cannot make rights scope, territory, term, media, exclusivity, licensor or licensee legal as creative facts.

No external legal rule, jurisdiction-specific doctrine, statutory form list, numeric threshold or confidence score was introduced. The memo uses ordinary document structures as evidence: party recitals, operative grants, consent clauses, execution blocks, reporting tables, payment totals, manifests and asset identifiers. Whether a particular informal email forms an enforceable licence is deliberately left to legal interpretation; this file-organizer may recognize that the email needs review, but it may not decide the law.

## Node test, argued in full

### 1. Does this row have distinct activation evidence?

The label has recognizable documents, but their recognition is not creative-specific. A photography usage licence, sync licence, copyright assignment, model release, property release and location release are executed-instrument shapes: named parties, operative grant or consent language, defined scope, and an execution block. Those are exactly the kind of structural signals that activate the `legal` safety schema. The creative work title, campaign, thumbnail, asset identifier or embedded preview tells us what the instrument is about. It does not transform the instrument into a separate creative schema.

Royalty statements and usage reports have a different shape: issuer, reporting period, work identifiers, units or exploitations, territories, gross amounts, deductions, withholding and net payable. That structure activates `finance`. The work identifier is again a subject or line-item value. It does not make the accounting record a creative template.

The strongest apparent signal for this node—words such as licence, release, royalty, rights and usage—is never-alone. The same vocabulary appears in open-source licence notices, stock-provider receipts, boilerplate terms, rights-clearance matrices, casual email and legal instruments. An extension is also never sufficient. A project folder supplies neighbourhood, not an extraction licence. The activation leg therefore points outward to existing schemas rather than inward to a creative node.

### 2. Does this row have a distinct recommended dimension order?

The tempting order is work or agreement → grant type → territory → term → exploitation medium. Every meaningful level after the first is a rights-grant fact. The creative schema declares none of them. The first level is not safe either: “work” might be a title, campaign, asset id, project, catalogue entry or agreement. A person or organization at the top would be a licensor/licensee role, and authorship or party identity must not become a destination merely because it is available.

An empty dimension order is therefore not a missing piece to improvise; it is evidence that the template cannot legally exist on the current schema. If the later legal-schema decision creates grant-specific fields, the legal template can recommend an order. If finance handles royalty records, its existing institution/account or record dimensions remain the lawful source. If a deliverable manifest organizes exported assets and checksums, the handoff situation can recommend its own dimensions once licensed. Combining those separate orders under “creative licensing” would recreate the old giant-category error.

### 3. Does this row have distinct privacy or handling rules?

The files are often sensitive: signed names, addresses, contact details, likeness consent, negotiated pricing, territory strategy, signatures, bank and tax information, and identity scans. However, sensitivity does not distinguish this node from `legal`, `finance` or `identity`. It reinforces their protection-first behavior. A separate creative template would not add a new safety rule; it would risk weakening an existing one by making an executed release look like routine project collateral.

All three legs fail to establish a creative-specific node. Activation belongs to legal or finance; dimensions require fields those schemas, not creative, must decide; privacy is inherited from safety neighbours. Refusal is therefore the positive result.

## The first eight concrete files

### 1. `Campaign-photography-usage-licence-executed.pdf`

`source_type`: `text_document`. Page one identifies a photography usage licence, the recital names photographer and brand, operative clauses label media, territory and term, and the late body contains signatures and execution dates. Universal facts such as file type, creation date, language and sensitivity status are legal. Rights scope, territory, term, exclusivity and party roles are not creative facts. The same bytes activate `legal`; if execution or content cannot be read, they fall to Protected Records or Review Later. A campaign name or photograph title alone does not prove the grant applies to a particular asset.

### 2. `Model Release - Jordan Lee - signed.pdf`

`source_type`: `text_document`. Heading, consent/likeness language, labelled releasor/producer lines and execution block form an operative release. The person name is sensitive and never a destination. An embedded headshot does not make the file a photo-event record, and the word signed in the filename does not prove execution without the content. This is legal material, with Protected Records as the residual when activation cannot safely proceed.

### 3. `Location Release - Harbor Warehouse.docx`

`source_type`: `text_document`. The file names property and production in labelled slots and contains permission language, but the signature block is blank. It may be a draft or precedent rather than an operative record. The organizer may recognize legal-form structure and send the file to Review Later; it must not conclude that permission was granted, or create a creative project from the property name. The collision is between instrument shape and status, not between two creative subtypes.

### 4. `Q2 2026 Sound Recording Royalty Statement.xlsx`

`source_type`: `spreadsheet`. The workbook has a reporting-period heading, issuer, track identifiers, usage quantities, territory codes, gross royalty, withholding and net payable. This is finance evidence. Territory and period here describe reported exploitation, not necessarily contractual scope. Payment does not prove copyright ownership. If finance does not activate, an isolated statement belongs in Receipts and Confirmations or Protected Records, never in a folder inferred from a track name alone.

### 5. `Getty Images licence receipt 8842103.pdf`

`source_type`: `text_document`. The document labels issuer, order number, purchase date, asset identifier and amount, with a short licence summary but no negotiated recital or execution block. Its transaction structure supports finance; its boilerplate may be reference evidence for permitted use, but it does not prove a negotiated legal matter or copyright ownership. The clean residual is Receipts and Confirmations. This fixture shows why the word licence cannot activate this row.

### 6. `Rights granted for Summer Campaign.eml`

`source_type`: `email`. Labelled sender, recipient and subject slots exist; body prose informally approves social use for a stated period; no executed attachment is present. This may be legally meaningful, but enforceability and authority require interpretation. The organizer can flag a possible legal record and keep it local; it may not promote prose into definitive rights facts. The campaign neighbourhood may group the message for review without copying permission facts onto assets.

### 7. `Talent-and-location-release-packet.zip`

`source_type`: `archive`. The manifest lists releases and identity scans, with a mix of signed and blank suffixes. Inspection must occur without extraction to disk. The outer archive cannot prove which members are operative. Identity members activate `identity` and immediate protection; instrument members may activate `legal`; the archive group remains reviewable. One outer filename cannot write one grant across all people and locations.

### 8. `Artwork usage rights matrix v7.pptx`

`source_type`: `presentation`. Slides list artworks against markets, channels and campaign dates; status cells say cleared, pending or expired; notes cite agreements. The matrix administers or summarizes rights but does not itself grant them. Version-shaped filename evidence supports a version-family observation, not acceptance of version seven. This belongs with legal review or contract administration, depending on holder context; it does not license a creative field set.

## Additional fixtures and ugly cases

`final_campaign_master_v12.psd` is the principal creative collision fixture. It is real creative evidence: native layers, linked assets, editing metadata and a version-shaped filename. None of those observations proves linked assets are cleared, final means delivered, or the work is licensed. It may group beside an agreement by project context without receiving the agreement's rights facts.

`LICENSE.txt` beside `package.json` and `src/` is the principal legal-vocabulary false positive. Permission, redistribution and warranty language are genuine, but repository structure supports `code`, and the absence of named negotiated parties or execution evidence prevents a personal legal-matter reading. It is not creative licensing simply because copyright is creative-adjacent.

`passport-scan-for-model-release.jpg` is the principal safety collision. OCR document labels and machine-readable structure support `identity`; camera metadata supports a capture; the filename's reference to a release supports neither consent nor execution. Protection must win before any creative-project summary or photo placement.

`deliverables_manifest.csv` is the principal handoff collision. Filename, checksum, format and delivery-status columns prove an asset-transfer manifest. A note saying rights documents were supplied separately does not prove those documents exist in the corpus, that copyright transferred, or that delivery itself granted use. Handoff and licensing are separate record functions even when the same archive contains both.

An unreadable `signed_release_bundle.zip` remains `Unsupported or Encrypted` or Protected Records. Filename inference is forbidden. A screenshot of an e-signature confirmation needs OCR/content evidence and is still only confirmation that a workflow event occurred, not proof of the complete instrument's terms. A blank release template downloaded for later use is a Reference Clip or Review Later item, not an operative grant.

## Proposed-field analysis

`proposed_fields` is intentionally empty. The obvious candidates are rights scope, grant type, exploitation medium, territory, term, exclusivity, agreement status, licensor, licensee, releasor, grantee, work identifier and royalty period. They were not proposed for three reasons.

First, the assignment is a template over `creative`, whose schema declares no fields. A template may not invent a second copy of another schema's vocabulary. Second, nearly all candidates belong to the legal instrument or finance report, not to the creative work. Third, the party fields create role-split questions that must be solved canonically: one organization may be creator, agency, licensor, licensee, client, distributor or payor. Minting local keys would hard-code the very role conflation the design forbids.

The universal fields remain available: `file_type`, `creation_date`, `language`, `duplicate_family`, `version_family`, and `sensitivity_status`. The JSON examples name only applicable universal keys in `facts_legal`; absence from an example does not revoke a universal field. No folder path appears as a fact.

## Reciprocal boundaries

### `legal`

Toward legal: party recitals, operative grant or consent clauses, defined rights and execution evidence activate legal. A creative work title, campaign, thumbnail or asset identifier is subject evidence only. Toward creative: a master, moodboard, brief or asset file stays creative when it contains no operative instrument. Same bytes in competition: `Campaign-photography-usage-licence-executed.pdf` and `Model Release - Jordan Lee - signed.pdf`. The refused node does not steal either.

### `finance`

Toward finance: issuer, reporting period, usage rows, amounts, withholding, invoice totals and payment advice activate finance. Toward legal: grant scope and obligations require an instrument. Toward creative: the track or image id remains subject matter. Same bytes: `Q2 2026 Sound Recording Royalty Statement.xlsx` and `Getty Images licence receipt 8842103.pdf`. The word royalty or licence is shared and discriminates nothing.

### `creative.client-engagement`

Toward client engagement: a brief, statement of work, review cycle, delivery obligation and client/project context describe doing the work. Toward legal: an operative rights clause describes permission or ownership. A client name and project title support neither node alone. Same bytes: a signed statement of work containing both deliverables and a rights clause. It may legitimately co-activate creative engagement and legal on disjoint evidence; this refused node is not the join.

### `creative.deliverable-handoff`

Toward handoff: exported masters, checksums, formats, transfer confirmation and acceptance evidence prove delivery. Toward legal: recitals, operative grant and execution prove permission. Delivery does not transfer copyright by itself; a licence does not prove files were delivered. Same bytes: `deliverables_manifest.csv` beside a signed licence in one handoff archive. Group membership does not copy facts between them.

### `identity`

Toward identity: bearer fields, document number, issuing authority and machine-readable structure identify protected identity material. Toward legal: consent and execution identify a release. A signed release names a person but is not an identity document; a passport scan stored for onboarding is not consent. Same bytes: `passport-scan-for-model-release.jpg` and the mixed release packet.

### `photos`

No edge was written to `photos`. A model release may contain a headshot, and an identity attachment may carry EXIF, but capture metadata and likeness-consent language are disjoint enough that the landed identity/legal safety boundaries already handle the risky bytes. A work being a photograph is a value/subject, not a reason for a licensing template. Adding a photos edge would suggest that every licensed image is a collision when most are simply referenced by identifier.

### `law_practice.contract-negotiation`

No edge was written. A lawyer's redline, issues list and advice correspondence organize the professional matter of negotiating language; the holder's executed licence is the resulting legal instrument. Matter identifiers, privilege/work-product framing and tracked negotiation artifacts discriminate the practitioner situation. This row is already refused to legal, so a second edge would duplicate the legal-practice boundary rather than clarify activation.

### `creative` schema default

No self-edge exists. The creative asset and the rights instrument may be grouped by a reviewable project relationship, but the subject's title or identifier must not be copied into a rights fact and the instrument's grant must not be copied onto every asset in the folder. The refused row therefore has no grouping reasons or dimensions.

## Files considered and rejected as evidence for this node

- A layered PSD, raw photo, audio master, exported video or font binary: the object being licensed, not evidence of the grant.
- A creative brief saying “global social campaign”: intended use, not permission from a rights holder.
- A statement of work naming deliverables and fees: client-engagement evidence unless operative rights language independently activates legal.
- A delivery manifest or checksum list: handoff evidence, not ownership or use permission.
- A royalty statement: finance evidence; paid exploitation does not reveal the complete legal grant.
- A stock-photo receipt: transaction evidence, often with boilerplate summary, but not a creative schema.
- A code repository `LICENSE.txt`: repository/legal notice, not a negotiated creative engagement.
- A blank model-release form: precedent or pending paperwork, not executed consent.
- A passport scan beside releases: identity evidence requiring protection, not consent.
- A rights-clearance matrix: administrative assertion pointing to instruments, not the grant itself.
- A campaign folder containing a licence: useful neighbourhood for reviewable grouping, never proof that every member is covered.
- A filename containing `signed`, `final`, `approved`, `licensed`, `royalty` or a territory: possible clue only.

## Residual routing

Protected Records is primary for signed releases, negotiated licences, identity attachments and sensitive royalty material when safe activation or placement is unavailable. Independent Records holds durable standalone notices or instruments with no reliable group. Receipts and Confirmations holds isolated stock-asset purchase receipts and payment confirmations. Review Later holds incomplete, unsigned, informal or contradictory rights evidence. Reference Clips holds blank forms, model wording and downloaded licence references. Unsupported or Encrypted represents unreadable bundles without forcing them open or inferring purpose from filenames.

These are residual names, not schemas and not substitutes for legal or finance activation. A file may remain protected while its schema evidence is reviewed.

Alternative A minimizes schemas and treats contract linkage as graph/group evidence. Alternative B permits legal/finance co-activation on disjoint clauses and payment rows. Neither licenses a creative schema today.

**NJ-CLR-3 — informal grants.** Should an unexecuted email saying “you may use this for three months” be surfaced as possible legal material under local review, or remain Review Later until an instrument or explicit user confirmation exists? The organizer must not decide enforceability. This affects recognition policy, not the refusal verdict.

## End-state claims and consistency check

The JSON and memo make the same claim: `creative.licensing-rights` is refused because it combines legal instruments, finance records and creative subjects without a creative-specific field set. Both leave `fields` and `proposed_fields` empty, recommend no dimensions, set potentially-sensitive handling, and route the same document functions to legal, finance and named residuals. The first eight JSON examples are the first eight memo fixtures in the same order. Additional collision fixtures are also mirrored.

The refusal preserves the useful distinction instead of deleting coverage: rights grants and releases remain detectable through legal evidence; royalty and payment records remain detectable through finance evidence; creative masters remain creative; identity attachments remain protected; handoff manifests remain delivery evidence. No work type, file extension, person name, organization name, asset title, project neighbourhood or signature suffix activates the refused row alone.

Final claim: the honest product behavior is to recognize the record's function and retain graph relationships to the creative work, not to invent a creative licensing category. That is a complete reroute, not a gap.
