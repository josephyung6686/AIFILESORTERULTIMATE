# Research memo — `law_practice.pro-bono`

Date: 2026-08-26
Depth: J-DEPTH
Output: `planning/domains/nodes/law_practice.pro-bono.json`
Roster row: template on the fieldless `law_practice` schema, `parent_id: null`, placeholder launch
Result: **ACCEPTED NARROWLY.** Roughly two thirds of the row as the roster hint describes it is refused inside the accepted node and routed away by name.

## The charge, argued first and at its strongest

Before anything was written, the case that this row should not exist was constructed as strongly as it could be. It is a good case. Four of the charge's named failure modes apply to it simultaneously, and one of them nearly killed it.

**(1) It is a value in a slot the schema already carries.** `law_practice`'s `work_types` enum contains, verbatim, `"engagement terms, retainer, scope and funding-basis record"`. Pro bono, legal aid, conditional fee, insurance-backed and privately retained are the values that go in the funding-basis slot of that one record. A row whose whole content is one value of one slot of one document is the 574's original mistake. This is not hypothetical here: the sibling `law_practice.engagement-terms` was **refused** for a closely related reason — it "IS that schema's activation precondition" — and pro bono is a *narrower* slice of the same artefact than engagement-terms was. If engagement-terms could not survive, the funding-basis value inside it should not either.

**(2) It is defined by the absence of something.** "Pro bono" means no fee was charged. An absent bill is not a structure, and the design's own instance of this error — refusing missing metadata as proof of screen origin — rules it out directly. Concretely: `law_practice.time-and-billing` fires on a matter-reference column beside timekeeper, narrative, duration and rate. A pro bono timesheet is that identical table with the rate cell empty. An empty cell cannot be a detection signal.

**(3) Its most obvious evidence is never-alone.** Legal Aid Agency, Legal Services Corporation, Legal Aid Ontario, a law-school clinic, a bar clearinghouse, a public defender office — every one is an organisation name, struck by the schema's deletion test and independently struck by 00's template validation, which rejects a template that would "use an author or organization merely as a collector". A reference to a professional-responsibility rule on voluntary service is a document-type word wearing a number.

**(4) It duplicates neighbours in every direction.** The eligibility interview looks like `law_practice.client-intake`'s. The funding record looks like the refused `law_practice.engagement-terms`'. The hours report looks like `business_operations`'. The grant return looks like `government.grant-programme-administration`'s counterparty. The fee claim looks like `law_practice.time-and-billing`'s. Subtract all four and there is a plausible reading on which nothing at all is left.

The row was carried to the point of refusal on this basis and then re-tested. What defeats the charge is not that the case is weak. It is that the charge attacks *funding as an attribute of a matter*, and there is a second thing under the same roster name that the charge does not reach.

## What defeats it

**The schema's default is a two-role structure. This row is a three-role structure, and the third role is a party to nothing.**

`law_practice`'s default requires two legs: an exact matter, file or engagement reference repeated across two or more artefacts, and at least one artefact whose own labelled slots separate a practitioner or firm role from a client role. Every sibling inherits that. What publicly funded work introduces is a **funder** who is neither the practitioner nor the client, is not a party to the matter, has no relationship with the opposing side, and yet **decides whether the work may be done, limits its scope, caps its cost, sets its rates, assesses the bill afterwards, and can recoup from what the client recovers**. No other role in the entire `law_practice` family behaves like that. A client instructs and pays. A court adjudicates. A counterparty opposes. A funder authorises — which is a distinct verb and it leaves distinct labelled slots on distinct real documents.

The four structures that carry it, with real named document types rather than genres:

- **The eligibility determination.** England and Wales criminal legal aid uses **CRM14** (the application) with **CRM15** as a separate financial statement; the civil scheme has its application and means assessment. Legal Aid Ontario issues certificates on a certificate application. A United States **Legal Services Corporation** grantee screens applicants against income and asset criteria at intake. The shared shape is decisive and exists nowhere else in this family: a client's income, disposable income, capital, dependants and passporting-benefit slots on one side; a **determination outcome** and a **contribution or nil-contribution figure** on the other; a determining officer and date. This is 00's direct-fact path — "a labeled form field" — applied to slots that are about a person's poverty.
- **The issued authority.** A legal aid **certificate**, a criminal **representation order**, a **prior authority** to instruct an expert above scheme rate. Its slots: an **authority-allocated reference that is demonstrably not the firm's own matter reference** (both appear on the same page and differ in format), a **scope limitation**, a **cost limitation**, an issuing body, an effective date, and an amendment chain. This is the only artefact anywhere in `law_practice` in which a non-party controls what work may be done.
- **The third-party-payer claim.** A costs claim to the funder (**CIV CLAIM1**, **CRM7** and their equivalents), assessed at prescribed rates, returned with allowed and reduced figures and reason codes; and the **statutory charge** notice, in which the funder recoups from the client's recovery. The payee is the practice; the payer is a body that is not a party; the rates were not agreed with anybody; and the outcome column was written by someone outside the practice.
- **The gateway filing.** Civil legal aid in England and Wales requires prescribed **evidence of domestic abuse** or of child abuse to open the gateway for family funding. Its structure is a third-party attestation citing the **scheme's evidence criterion by paragraph** rather than the merits of the claim.

Strike every entity name and every document-type word from those four, as the schema's deletion test requires, and what survives is: a determination outcome, a contribution figure, an authority-allocated reference distinct from the matter reference, a scope and cost limitation, a prescribed-rate assessment column, and a criterion citation. That is a structure. It survives the deletion test that kills the word "pro bono".

## The node test, all three legs

**Leg one — detection signals differ.** The schema default fires on two roles plus a repeated matter reference. This row requires that *plus* an artefact whose own labelled slots carry the funder as a role, expressed structurally. The relationship is strictly additive and strictly narrower, which is the correct shape for a template: it can never fire where the schema does not, and it fires on a proper subset. Critically, the added leg is not a value test — the row does **not** ask whether a funding-basis field says "legal aid". It asks whether a determination, an allocated reference, a limitation, or a claim addressed outward exists as structure. That is the difference between a value and a node, and it is the whole of this row's claim to exist.

**Leg two — privacy rules differ, and this is the strongest leg.** The `law_practice` schema's stated privacy ground is that the exposed party is a **third party** who never chose this filesystem and cannot consent. This row is stricter along an axis the schema does not name. Its characteristic artefacts do not merely *identify* that third party; they record a **means-test outcome about them** — a disposable-income figure, a passporting benefit, a capital total, a contribution they could not afford — and through the gateway structure, sometimes their status as an abuse survivor, an asylum claimant, or the subject of a child-protection concern. 00's corpus sentence names exactly this material: the product processes a corpus that "can include identity documents, account statements, tax records, medical information, legal records, credentials, private correspondence, GPS metadata, employment materials, and educational records". The operative posture is 00's own — "The default posture must therefore be local-first and data-minimizing." — and the display rule is 00's own example: "A summary such as “11 protected identity records” may be safe to show, while a visible list of passport filenames on a shared screen may not be." Here even the *summary* fails if it is bucketed by funding basis, because the bucket is the disclosure.

**Leg three — the dimension recommendation differs, by a prohibition the schema does not state.** Both orders are empty under PR-6, so the difference lives in the prose, and it is a real one. The schema recommends client (rarely, and only on explicit approval) → matter → function → period, and forbids a **named third party** as a level because a path writes their identity where every later process reads it. This row extends that prohibition from the third party's **name** to an **attribute** of them: *the funding basis may never be a folder level*. A branch called `Legal Aid` or `Pro Bono` says "this client was assessed and found poor enough", which is a means-test disclosure the schema's name-only rule does not currently prevent. A second, independent reason converges on the same answer from 00's template validation, which rejects a template that would "create meaningless one-child levels": in a wholly legally-aided practice the level is universal and carries no information; in a mixed practice it is a disclosure. There is no corpus in which it is correct. Not time-first, for the schema's inherited reason — "For document and record domains, project, function, or subject usually comes before time because putting year first scatters related work across calendar folders." — and because application, grant, event, claim and assessment dates all mean different things here.

Two legs differ substantively and the third differs by an added rule. The node test is passed, narrowly and on the record.

## The collision fixture

**`Costs estimate and funding options - 41127-0006.xlsx`** — and it is deliberately taken verbatim from the landed `law_practice.engagement-terms` row's own file list, so that the same fixture is named on both sides.

It is the perfect false positive. It is a spreadsheet, in a matter folder, with a column headed **Legal aid**, populated with a scheme rate and a note reading "subject to means and merits". Every keyword this row could be tempted by is present. It is **not** this row's evidence, and the discriminator is exact: the workbook **offers** funding options and **grants** none. There is no determination, no outcome, no contribution figure, and no authority-allocated reference anywhere in it. It falls to Review Later. This is the row's defining discipline: the offered/granted distinction is what separates a funding *topic* from a funding *structure*, and the row refuses the topic.

## Files considered and rejected

- **`Conditional Fee Agreement - no win no fee - executed.pdf`** — the second-best false friend, and arguably more seductive than the first, because it genuinely is free at the point of use. Rejected twice over: there is no third role (the payer is the client, out of a recovery), and it has a bound party pair plus an execution block, which is `legal`'s executed-instrument signal, and `legal` is a safety domain whose protection runs first — "Finance, identity, medical, and legal material should be implemented first as safety domains, meaning the system detects and protects them before any cloud or automated placement decision is allowed."
- **A fee-free timesheet** — a matter-reference column with a blank or zero rate. Rejected as pure absence. It stays with `law_practice.time-and-billing`.
- **`Pro Bono Committee - annual hours by attorney - FY2026.xlsx`**, plus firm pro bono policies, pledges, targets and annual-report narratives. Rejected: the rows are attorneys, not matters; the subject is the firm describing itself; the deletion test leaves an organisation name and a document-type word. These are `business_operations`' and `hr`'s.
- **`LSC grantee report - CSR case service data - 2026.zip`** — rejected as this row's *anchor*, kept only as a fixture showing where the line falls. An organisation's return about its own grant performance is grant administration seen from the recipient end.
- **`Source of Funds - Vance - statements Jan to Jun 2026.pdf`** — taken verbatim from `law_practice.client-intake`'s own list. Rejected: financial documents about a client held by a practice are not a means assessment. See the reciprocal below.
- **A clinic seminar or coursework exercise in eligibility screening, marked by a tutor.** Rejected; it is education material and the marking is the tell. The `law_practice` schema already carries the analogous fixture (`LPC Skills - sample client attendance note (marked).docx`), so this row does not restate it as a fixture, only as a rejection.
- **A duty-scheme rota.** Kept as a fixture only to record that it does *not* activate: availability is not a matter and attendees are not clients.
- **A blank scheme application form downloaded from a portal.** Rejected: its determination and contribution slots are empty by design, which is the precedent-bank inverse-recognition case, not this row's.
- **A live legal aid billing system or case-management account.** Rejected as a node: a source system is not a file. A bounded export with a readable manifest is represented; connector ingestion is a later security decision.
- **Practice-area taxonomies, categories of law, scheme names, jurisdictions and funding-scheme lists.** Rejected under J-IND. Enumerating them would turn a placeholder into the industry catalogue this round defers.

## Reciprocal boundaries

Each is stated in both directions, and where the two sides genuinely compete the **same fixture is named on both sides**.

- **`law_practice.client-intake`** — shared fixture: *`Source of Funds - Vance - statements Jan to Jun 2026.pdf`*, that row's own. Both sides hold detailed financial documents about a person who is not the holder, collected by a practice; the financial *content* does not discriminate. The **purpose structure** does. Intake collects means to prove the money is **clean** — certification, verification, beneficial ownership, per-document attestation, ending in a disposition about whether to act. This row's means artefact proves the client is **poor enough** — a threshold, an outcome, a contribution — and it is addressed to a body outside the practice. **This row does not take that fixture.** Reciprocally, `law_practice.client-intake` should not take an eligibility form carrying an outcome and a contribution merely because it precedes the work and concerns a prospective client. Where one pack contains both, both activate on disjoint slots and neither copies the other's facts.
- **`law_practice.time-and-billing`** — shared fixture: *`Claim for costs - CIV CLAIM1 - assessed with reductions.pdf`*. Discriminator: payer role and rate provenance. An issuer-and-billed-to invoice whose billed-to is the **client**, at **agreed** rates, is that row's. A claim whose payer is a **non-party** body, at **prescribed** rates, with an assessment outcome the practice did not control, is this row's. Reciprocally, a fee-free timesheet stays with that row and does not come here; and a scheme claim does not become that row's merely because it tabulates hours.
- **`legal`** — shared fixture: *`Conditional Fee Agreement - no win no fee - executed.pdf`*, which is `legal`'s. Reciprocally, a legal aid certificate is **not** `legal`'s: no caption, no bound party pair, no execution block, so `legal`'s instrument-and-proceeding signals do not fire on it at all. That negative space is precisely what makes it available to `law_practice`.
- **`finance.student-financial-aid`** — the structural twin and the sharpest test of the row. Its core artefact is a household means assessment with income, assets, dependants, a threshold, an award outcome and an expected contribution — slot-for-slot the shape of a legal aid means form. Discriminator: **whose household, and in whose file**. That row assesses the **holder**; this row's assessment is about a **client**, held by a practitioner, beside an authority-issued permission to do legal work. Reciprocally, a legal aid means form must not route there on its income slots, and a student aid determination must not route here on the word *contribution*. If the **holder** is themselves an aided client, neither fires — that is `legal.personal-legal-matters`', because the practitioner role is absent.
- **`government.grant-programme-administration`** — shared fixture: *`LSC grantee report - CSR case service data - 2026.zip`*. That row holds the **authority's** side: programme design, grantee selection, monitoring. This row holds only the recipient practitioner's side and only where the artefact is matter-anchored. Reciprocally, a certificate issued for one named client's matter is not grant administration merely because a public body issued it.
- **`business_operations.organisational-records`** — shared fixture: *`Pro Bono Committee - annual hours by attorney - FY2026.xlsx`*, which is theirs. Reciprocally, a controlled-work matter-start schedule whose **rows are matters** with authority references is not an organisational record even though it is filed under a contract.
- **`law_practice.immigration-casework`** — heavy real-world overlap, because the funded caseload and the immigration caseload are largely the same files. Only the eligibility determination, the issued authority, the claim and the gateway filing are this row's; the substantive claim record is that row's whether or not it is funded. Neither absorbs the other's whole file.

## Neighbours considered that did not get an edge

- **`law_practice.engagement-terms`** — no edge, because the row is refused and its coverage already routes to the schema default. The seam is recorded in prose instead: "free" is a value in the funding-basis slot of the artefact that row was refused for.
- **`law_practice.conflicts-check`, `law_practice.deadlines-diary`, `law_practice.court-filing-record`** — funded matters produce all three, but on their own unchanged evidence. Funding does not alter a conflict search or a limitation diary, so there is no same-evidence mutex.
- **`legal.practice-matter-file`** — the sibling marker on the `legal` schema. Its recommendation is one shallow protected packet with no internal depth, which this row does not contradict; the seam is already argued at schema level and restating it here would duplicate the anchor.
- **`government.social-services-casework`** — a benefits eligibility determination made by an authority is structurally close, but the holder there is the authority, not a practitioner acting for the applicant. Flagged for R1c rather than asserted as a mutex, since that row was not read.
- **`identity`, `medical`, `finance`** — co-activations, not collisions; recorded in `also_holds_with`, each one-way with the reciprocal owed to R1c.

## proposed_fields

**Empty.** The `law_practice` schema declares no field rows under PR-6, and a template may not mint what its schema does not declare. Concepts that would be needed if D1 lifts — a funding authority, an authority-allocated reference, a determination outcome, a contribution, a scope or cost limitation — are all deliberately withheld and surfaced as NJ-PB-2 instead. The canonical role pair `our_firm` / `client` is inherited through the schema's `role_split` and is recorded there as **insufficient**: it has no third seat for a payer who is a party to nothing.

`proposed_context_terms` are proposed, not claimed as design. 00's academic-context floor is not asserted to cover this vocabulary.

## NEEDS-JOSEPH

1. **NJ-PB-1 — the existence question.** Answered here as a narrow yes and recorded so R1c can reverse it cheaply. If a funder is judged to be a *payer value* on an engagement rather than a *role in a structure*, the correct outcome is refusal, and the coverage routes to `law_practice` (schema default), `legal`, `law_practice.time-and-billing`, `law_practice.client-intake`, `business_operations`, `hr`, `government.grant-programme-administration` and `legal.personal-legal-matters`, plus Protected Records and Review Later. This row would rather be refused than kept as a scheme label.
2. **NJ-PB-2 — the third-role key.** `our_firm` and `client` cannot express a payer who is neither. Alternatives: mint a funder/payer role key and seed it destination-ineligible; extend `client` with a payer sub-role; or hold the gap open. This row mints nothing.
3. **NJ-PB-3 — the scope of the attribute prohibition.** This row asserts that a third party's *assessed means* is as unsafe in a path as their *name*, extending a rule the schema states only for names. That generalises well beyond this family — a diagnosis, an immigration status, a benefit, a protected characteristic. Alternatives: ratify it product-wide as a destination rule; keep it local to this row; or reject it and rely on the named-third-party rule alone, which would leave a `Legal Aid` branch permissible. A **cross-row recommendation for R1c**, not a change this row may make.
4. **NJ-PB-4 — the reporting seam.** The matter-start schedule and the case-service report sit exactly on the line between matter-anchored artefact and organisational return. This row draws it at *whether the rows are matters* rather than attorneys, periods or programmes, and asks R1c to land it reciprocally with `business_operations.organisational-records` and `government.grant-programme-administration`.
