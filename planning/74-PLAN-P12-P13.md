## 1. What each part is for

### P12 — apply and undo

P12 is the only part that mutates the filesystem. In the design's own sentence, the product
*"should treat every filesystem mutation as a transaction with preconditions, execution
steps, verification, and a reversible journal entry"*, and *"no file should move merely
because a placement score is high"* (`00`:155). It receives an approved placement decision,
resolves the destination node to a real path, evaluates the complete expected precondition
twice, applies one action at a time under the four checksum verification points (`00`:153),
and appends a journal entry that makes the move conditionally reversible. Its second job is
that reversal: undo is *"conditional rather than destructive"*, and where the disk no longer
matches the journal the product must be able to say *"This action cannot be undone
automatically because the file changed after it was moved"* and hand over the paths and the
hashes rather than force a rollback (`00`:175). P12 has no opinion about where a file
belongs. It applies, or it refuses, and it records which.

### P13 — the review and approval surface

P13 is the surface on which a person sees what the system decided and says what should
happen instead. It *"renders records other parts publish and collects user actions"*; it
decides nothing, validates nothing and mutates nothing (P13 SPEC:12-14). It exists because
eight obligations in `00` describe a user-facing moment no other part owns: the trust
distinction between a direct and a context-supported placement (§6.11), the residual
surfacing screen *"rather than presenting a single intimidating pile"* (§7.5), the set-level
decision that must precede any per-file model call (§7.6), an editable recommendation with
bulk actions (§7.10), the plan shown *"where policy requires review"* and the stale-plan and
undo-conflict moments (§8.3), the redaction settings and the four consent options (§8.4),
the user-facing evaluation view (§8.5), the completed-versus-deferred progress line (§8.6),
the scope choice on every correction and the inspect-and-reset surface (§8.7), and the
plan-version diff (§8.8). Without P13, §8.3's `Required review policy` field has no consumer
at all: a plan can be marked review-required and nothing exists that can review it.

retention default) is P12's contract, not automatic filing's.

**The caveat that must not be lost.** `69` §1 records nine of ten gate conditions green and
**G10 — the persona re-run — failing**. `68-PERSONA-RERUN.md` measured four corpora through
the shipped command: 0 files ready to file, a one-folder tree, 26 of 26 files waiting.

That instruction is right about P12 and **only partly right about P13**, and this is the one
place this document argues with `69`. Two of G10's three named blockers are P13's:

Both are review-surface holes. `cli.py:613-645` says so in its own docstring — the merge

### 4.3 What P13 must refuse

- **To construct any action over `untouched_protected`.** Protected containers are marked and
  counted and carry no action at all (`69` §0; P13 SPEC:260-262).
- **To compute a percentage or ratio from `ProtectedSummary.count` and `class_breakdown`.**
  They are different denominators (D11).
- **To map `NeedsConsent` to `abstain`.** A pending consent request renders as
  `blocked_pending_user`, which is already a live member of `REVIEW_POLICIES` (B2).
- **To render a resolved filesystem path** anywhere except the four §8.3 path fields the
  apply and undo-conflict items themselves demand (B3).
- **To ask for a filename, preview, thumbnail, OCR text or location the redaction policy
  withheld.** Redaction happens in the owning part; P13 has no path that receives protected
  content and then hides it (P13 SPEC:44-48).
- **To score, classify, validate, resolve a path or mutate anything** (Done-means 22).

## 5. Six findings from grounding on today's checkout

Each of these narrows or closes a flag standing in one of the two PLANs. **Each is a
departure from that PLAN as written, and is stated as one.**

### 5.1 `Node.existing_path` narrows P12's F1 — the root-anchor gap is smaller than the PLAN says

P12's PLAN F1 reads: *"`root_anchor` has no published path … Nothing in `src/` maps one to
the other, so the SPEC's 'a `root_anchor` names a §1.1 candidate root … so it has a real
path' has a consumer and no producer."* That is true of `root_anchor` and it is **not the
whole picture**. `Node.existing_path` is a real, observed filesystem path, carried on every
`existing` node and refused on every other node type
(`src/tree_design/records.py:164, 208-213`). `src/cli.py:1661-1712` already builds
`{node_id: existing_path}` and uses it to say whether a file is already in the folder the
plan names.

**Departure:** P12 Task 3 should resolve a node by walking the ancestor chain to the nearest
ancestor that is `existing` with a non-`None` `existing_path`, and compose the remaining
`display_label` chain beneath it under §8.3's normalization rules. `root_anchor` is consulted
**only** when no ancestor on the chain is `existing` — a tree entirely of proposed branches
under a root the user named but the product never observed. F1 remains open for that case and
only that case, and it is smaller: on the four corpora `68` measured, every adopted branch has
an `existing` ancestor because `cli.py` adopts the person's folders as `existing` nodes
carrying their real paths.

### 5.2 P12's F13 is CLOSED — `refused move` exists

P12 PLAN F13 reads: *"§8.2 has no event type for a refused or paused action, and Done-means
13 requires one. Minting a seventh is a spec-level act P12 may not perform."* It has since
been minted. `src/database_agent/events.py:47-54` carries **twenty** reserved types, and the
twentieth is `refused move`; `:30-44` records that the owner approved it on 2026-08-29, on
the grounds that a closed set which cannot express what actually happened is a design gap
rather than a discipline, and notes that **P12 is its only intended writer and it has none
yet**.

**Departure:** P12 authors **seven** event types, not six. Every refusal in §3.3 above
appends `refused move`. The `EVENT_FIELDS` are already sufficient — `explanation` carries the
distinct refusal language `66` §10 requires — and no schema change is needed.

### 5.3 P12's F15 is narrowed — the permitting-policy *predicate* exists; the *setting surface* does not

P12 PLAN F15 reads: *"§8.4's 'user policy that explicitly permits it' has no producer anywhere
in `src/`. `movement_permitted_for` is injected with no default."* The predicate is live and
complete: `privacy.moves.may_move_automatically(conn, file_id, plan_version)` returns a
`MoveVerdict(allowed, reason, permitting_policy)` over four reasons, checks absence **first**,
reads the flag rather than the class, reads the policy at the asked-for plan version, and
treats no policy at all as no permission (`src/privacy/moves.py:47-113`).

**Departure:** P12 Task 10 calls `privacy.moves.may_move_automatically` directly rather than
inventing an injected `movement_permitted_for` seam. What genuinely has no producer is the
**surface that writes `Policy.automatic_move_permissions`** (`src/privacy/policy.py:134`) —
i.e. the moment a person grants the permission. That is P13's, and it is not in P13's twenty
tasks. It is added as Wave B task B9 below. Until it exists, `may_move_automatically` refuses
every protected file, which is the correct posture and not a bug.

### 5.4 P12's F12 is decided by the code — P7's store is authoritative, and the disagreement is recordable

P12 PLAN F12: `review_policy` and `handling_class` reach P12 twice, from P11's decision and
from P7's `ClassificationStore.current(...)`, with no rule for a disagreement.

**Ruling, and it is a reading of existing code rather than a new decision:**
`may_move_automatically` already resolves the classification through
`ClassificationStore(conn).current(file_id, content_hash)` at call time
(`src/privacy/moves.py:101`), so P12 gets P7's *current* answer by construction. Where
`PlacementDecision.privacy.handling_class` disagrees with it, the decision is **stale**, not
wrong: the classification was superseded after the decision was made, which is exactly one of
§8.3's five triggers. P12 refuses with the staleness class and appends `refused move`; it does
not pick a winner. Recorded here so no implementer invents a precedence rule.

### 5.5 `LEAVE_UNTOUCHED` and `PRESERVE` — P12 is **not** their consumer. P13 is.

`tree_design.vocabulary.EXISTING_FOLDER_ACTIONS` publishes §5.10's six gestures a person has
over a folder they made — `preserve`, `adopt-as-branch`, `merge-with-proposal`,
`attach-beneath`, `rename-proposal-to-match`, `leave-untouched`
(`src/tree_design/vocabulary.py:393-406`). Today **nothing reads them.** They are not among
`store.TREE_EDIT_ACTIONS`' fifteen, and `placement.vocabulary.ACTION_LEAVE_UNTOUCHED`
(`src/placement/vocabulary.py:312`) is a different value — `"leave_untouched"` with an
underscore — belonging to P11's own review actions over a *file*, not a folder.
`src/cli.py:1496-1510` names the gap in the report a person reads: *"none of the six has a
consumer, so this command takes the only one it can defend with nobody at the screen."*

**Ruling: they are canvas gestures, collected by P13 and applied by P10, and P12 never sees
one.** They are decided at tree-design time and are settled by freeze. Two reasons this is
not close: (a) a §5.10 gesture changes the *tree*, and every tree change is
`tree_design.store.apply_review_action`'s, which P12 may not call (A5 forbids P12 editing any
P10 file); (b) by the time P12 runs, the outcome of the gesture is already encoded in the
frozen tree as `node_type`, `accepts_placement` and `existing_path` — a folder the person left
untouched is an `ignored` node, and `derive_accepts_placement` returns False for `ignored`
*precisely because* §5.10 guarantees a user may leave an existing folder untouched
(`src/tree_design/records.py:66-70`). P12 reads the flag and re-derives nothing.

**Consequence, and it is the actionable half:** the six gestures need a *writer*, and that
writer is P13's canvas collection. `TREE_EDIT_ACTIONS` has fifteen members and none of them
is one of the six, so P13's canvas surface cannot today route a `leave-untouched` anywhere.
Reconciling the two vocabularies is **an owner question** (§8 Q4) and neither this document
nor either PLAN answers it.

### 5.6 The passport-number label is a joint task, and it is in neither PLAN

`69` §3 blocker 3: a client's passport number became a group's `display_label` and, under
per-group acceptance, printed as a proposed **folder name**. It is filed as "P13 + P12" and
neither plan has a task for it. P13 Task 13 (the redaction boundary) is adjacent but is about
what P13 *asks for*, not about what a label already contains.

**Added:** Wave A task A6 (P13 side — a proposed label derived from protected material is
never presented as a folder name) and Wave C task C4 (P12 side — path composition refuses a
segment whose source node carries a protected `handling_class`, and appends `refused move`).
Both are additions to the existing PLANs and are marked as such.

## 9. P13, the CLI report, and `--answer`

Today `src/cli.py` stands in for P13 in three places, and each one is a different kind of
stand-in. Naming which is which matters, because two of them should survive P13 and one
should not.

**1. `report(...)` (`src/cli.py:1599-1831`) is a real, keepable rendering — of a subset.** It
already does several things P13's contract demands and does them well: protected containers
first and never folded into a total (`:1621-1629`); the person's own folders counted
separately from proposals (`:1637-1641`); a placement's headline taken from its review policy
rather than its outcome; the reason printed for every outcome that is not a placement; and
`"Nothing was moved."` as the last line (`:1830`). P13 does not replace this. P13 supplies the
**records** it renders — the review item, the location states, the progress line — and this
function becomes one renderer of them. The contract is P13's; the terminal is one surface.

**2. `--answer` (`src/cli.py:1868-1874`) is a real collection channel and should survive.** It
is P15's question surface: a question raised by the database, an option id typed back, `skip`
and `revoke` as first-class answers, and `AnswerRefused` when the flag names something the
database never asked about (`:1288-1320`). That is exactly the shape of a `review_action` —
a subject, a chosen option, a scope — collected without a GUI. **Wave A4's `review_action`
should be reachable through `--answer`**, and Wave A5 is the first use of it: one answer per
P9 group instead of one `--label` for all of them. This is the cheapest possible P13 client
and it is already built.

**3. `DEFAULTED_DECISIONS` (`src/cli.py:1485-1533`) is the honest confession, and it is the
part that should shrink.** It prints, under *"Decisions made for you, because nobody was at
the screen to ask"*, exactly the decisions a review surface exists to collect: which of the
person's folders to keep and how (all six §5.10 gestures, none with a consumer), which nesting
to use out of the ones the files support, and how deep each folder goes. Every entry P13
closes should be **deleted from this tuple in the same commit that closes it** — that is the
measurable definition of P13 landing. An entry that stays is a gap the person is still being
told about honestly, which is the correct behaviour and not a failure.

One thing the CLI cannot stand in for: **`review_approval`**. `--answer` can carry a gesture,
but §8.3's gate needs a typed record that names the plan, the decision, the plan version, the
policy that demanded review and what was shown (`presented_state_ref`). P12 SPEC:207-215 is
explicit that absence is a refusal and that there is no configuration which skips the check.
So the gate stays shut until Wave G2, and until then `src/mutation/` moves nothing that
required review. That is the intended state, not a blocker.
